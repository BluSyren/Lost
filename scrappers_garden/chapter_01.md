# Chapter 1: The Scavenger and the Seed

The air in Sector 4 tasted like copper and old dust. Elara adjusted the seal on her rebreather, wincing as the display on her wrist flickered. *14% Oxygen Reserves. Estimate: 3 hours.*

Three hours to find something worth selling, or she’d be breathing the unfiltered smog of the lower levels again, choking on the particulate matter that coated everyone’s lungs in black film.

Around her, the Heap rose like jagged mountains of oxidized metal. Twisted girders, shattered hull plates, and the skeletal remains of machines that had ceased to function centuries ago. This was the world: a graveyard of industry.

"Purge the broken," a voice boomed from the speakers mounted on the distant city walls. "Cast the flawed into the Pit. Only the pristine are worthy."

Elara kept her head down, her gloved hands sifting through a pile of corroded gears. The Rust-Walkers were out in force today. She could see their torches flickering in the haze below, a procession of red robes moving towards the Great Pit. They were singing their dirge, dragging a cart of "cursed" items—anything that had stopped working, anything that required repair. To fix was to defy entropy, and entropy was the will of the Cosmos, or so they preached.

She found a servo-motor that wasn't completely seized. It was small, barely worth a half-hour of air credits, but it was something. She shoved it into her canvas satchel.

She climbed higher, scaling a precarious ridge of pressed junk cars. The wind was stronger here, whipping her ragged coat around her legs. She was looking for the older strata, the layers of trash dumped before the Rust-Walkers took control, before the laws against tinkering were written in blood.

Something caught the weak sunlight filtering through the smog.

It wasn't the dull orange of rust or the grey of steel. It was... clear.

Elara shimmied across a beam, her boots finding purchase on the rivets. Tucked inside the crushed cabin of an ancient transport vehicle was a small box. It wasn't metal. It looked like glass, but stronger. Poly-glass?

She reached for it, her heart hammering against her ribs. If a Rust-Walker saw her holding this—an artifact of the Old World, clearly broken because it was here—they’d throw her into the Pit right along with it.

She wiped the grime from the surface. The box was sealed, airtight. Inside, resting on a bed of dried blue gel, was a small, brown pebble.

No, not a pebble. It was shaped like a tear, smooth and organic.

A seed.

Elara had only seen pictures of them in the forbidden data-caches. Organic life that grew from the ground, not manufactured in a vat. It looked dead. It looked like debris.

But as she held the box, a tiny green light blinked on the side of the container. *Stasis Failure. Critical.*

The box wasn't just a coffin; it was a life-support system, and it was failing.

"You there!"

Elara froze. Below her, on a walkway she hadn't checked, stood a Rust-Walker. His face was hidden behind a polished chrome mask, the only clean thing in the entire sector. He pointed a staff at her.

"What do you scavenge, rat?"

"Scrap!" Elara called back, her voice muffled by her mask. "Just seeking the broken to... to report it!"

"Bring it down. Let us inspect the curse."

Elara looked at the box in her hand. If she gave it to them, they would crush it. They would throw this tiny, impossible thing into the endless dark of the Great Pit.

She shoved the box deep into her coat, against her chest.

"I... I dropped it," she lied, backing away. "It fell. Into the crevices."

The Rust-Walker cocked his head. He raised a flare gun. "Lying is a fracture in the soul. Fractures must be purged."

He fired.

The flare hissed through the air, striking the metal beam a foot from her boot. Elara didn't wait. She scrambled up the pile, sliding on loose bolts and jagged shrapnel, disappearing over the ridge just as a second flare exploded where she had been standing.

She ran until her lungs burned and her oxygen warning beeped rapidly. She found shelter in a hollowed-out turbine, curling into a ball in the darkness.

Trembling, she pulled the box out. The light blinked faster now. *Critical. Critical.*

She looked at the brown seed. In a world of rust and death, it was the most beautiful, terrifying thing she had ever seen. She couldn't throw it away. She had to fix it.

And that made her a heretic.
