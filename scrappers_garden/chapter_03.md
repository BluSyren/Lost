# Chapter 3: The First Breath

Elara opened the door a crack. The chain held.

An Enforcer stood there, his face exposed but his armor piecemeal—scavenged plates painted with the red rust-symbol. He wasn't one of the true believers, just a hired thug for the cause.

"Power draw in this unit is irregular," he grunted, checking a datapad. "You running a heater? Illegal above Class 4."

"No heater," Elara said, her voice steady despite the adrenaline flooding her veins. "Just charging my rebreather packs. They're old. They draw hard."

The Enforcer narrowed his eyes. He leaned in, sniffing. "Smells like... ozone. And grease."

"I live in a scrap heap," Elara retorted. "Everything smells like grease."

He tried to push the door, but the chain held. "Let me see."

Elara reached into her pocket. She pulled out the servo-motor she’d found earlier. It was high-grade, better than anything on his armor.

"Found this," she said, dangling it in the gap. "Doesn't work for me. Maybe you can... dispose of it properly?"

The Enforcer’s eyes tracked the shiny metal. Greed warred with duty. Greed won. He snatched the motor.

"Fine. Fix your power draw. Next time, I break the door down."

He stomped away. Elara closed the door and leaned against it, sliding to the floor.

She couldn't stay here. He would sell the motor, buy booze, and then remember the weird hum in her room. He’d be back, or he’d tell a Priest for a reward.

She looked at the blanket-covered lump. She had to move. Now.

***

The "Forbidden Zone" was a legend among scavengers. Sector Zero. The core of the old city structure. The Rust-Walkers claimed it was the Maw of the Pit, a place of ultimate curse. But the blueprints Elara had studied suggested something else: an Arboretum.

Moving was a nightmare. She strapped the helmet-rig to her chest, swaddled in insulation. She carried the battery pack on her back. Every step was a calculation. Avoid the patrols. Avoid the crumbling floors.

She descended into the bowels of the city. The air grew thicker, heavier. The rust here was wet, slick with condensation.

She reached a massive blast door, welded shut ages ago. But Elara knew the secret. A maintenance hatch, hidden behind a pile of collapsed ventilation ducts.

She squeezed through, dragging her precious cargo.

Inside, it was silent.

She clicked on her torch. The beam cut through the darkness, revealing a cavernous space. Above, a domed ceiling made of dirty, reinforced glass looked up at the underbelly of the city levels above. No sunlight reached here, just the faint, ambient glow of the metropolis.

But the air... the air was different. Stagnant, yes, but not acrid.

She found a spot near a central pillar where old power conduits still hummed with faint residual energy. She set the rig down.

The battery was dying. The UV light flickered.

"No, no, no," she whispered.

She pulled out her splicing kit. She tore the cover off the pillar’s access panel. Live wires sparked. Dangerous.

"Forgive me," she said to the machine spirit the Rust-Walkers feared.

She hot-wired her rig into the city’s main grid.

The UV lamp surged to full brightness, blindingly white in the gloom. The fan in the helmet spun up with a confident whine.

Elara peered through the visor.

The nutrient paste had shifted during the journey. The seed was buried.

She waited. Minutes stretched into hours. She sat cross-legged on the cold floor, watching the helmet like a religious icon.

Had the journey killed it? Had the vibrations shaken it apart?

She checked the sensor display on the scrubber unit. CO2 levels inside the helmet were high. Oxygen: 0.1%.

She closed her eyes, exhausted. Maybe the Rust-Walkers were right. Maybe trying to fix things just prolonged the pain. Maybe she should just let go.

*Beep.*

A soft chirp from the sensor.

Elara opened her eyes.

Inside the helmet, the dirt had shifted. A tiny, pale hook had pushed its way up.

As she watched, it straightened. A stem. Thin as a wire, but green. A vivid, electric green that hurt her eyes in the best way possible.

The top of the stem unfurled. Two small leaves, no bigger than her fingernail, stretched out towards the UV light.

Elara glanced at the sensor.

*Oxygen: 0.2%.*

It wasn't much. It wasn't enough to keep a mouse alive, let alone a human. But it was rising.

The numbers ticked up. *0.21%... 0.22%...*

She pressed her hand against the glass. Inside that helmet, a tiny engine was working, taking the poison and turning it into life. A machine made of cells and water.

Elara smiled, tears cutting tracks through the grime on her face.

"Hello," she whispered to the sprout.

She was a criminal. A heretic. A scavenger of the cursed.

But as she sat in the dark, bathed in the purple glow of her creation, she breathed easier than she ever had in her life. The Garden had begun.
